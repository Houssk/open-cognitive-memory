# Vision and Scope

## Vision

Build an open, interdisciplinary research framework for understanding and engineering memory systems across human cognition and artificial intelligence.

OCM seeks to make memory-related concepts comparable, implementations inspectable, experiments reproducible, and datasets responsibly reusable.

## Problem

Research on memory is distributed across cognitive psychology, neuroscience, knowledge representation, information retrieval, machine learning, and agent engineering. The same terms can describe different mechanisms, while similar mechanisms may be described using different terminology.

This fragmentation makes it difficult to:
- compare human and artificial memory systems;
- evaluate memory structures beyond task-specific demonstrations;
- measure the effects of consolidation, association, and forgetting;
- reuse datasets and protocols across research groups;
- distinguish evidence from metaphor or implementation convention.

## Mission

OCM will develop:
1. A shared, versioned scientific vocabulary.
2. An ontology for concepts, associations, episodes, temporal context, and provenance.
3. Explicit hypotheses and research questions.
4. Reproducible human and machine data-collection protocols.
5. Open datasets with transparent governance.
6. Benchmarks for association, graph evolution, retrieval, consolidation, and continual learning.
7. An open process for review, contribution, and governance.

## Scope

### Included
- Human memory and cognitive organization, where research can be responsibly operationalized.
- Free association and semantic/episodic relationships.
- Dynamic and temporal knowledge graphs.
- Memory systems for LLM-based and other AI agents.
- Continual learning, consolidation, interference, and forgetting.
- Human–AI comparison under controlled protocols.
- Dataset and benchmark design.

### Out of scope for the initial phase
- Claims to reproduce the complete human brain or consciousness.
- Clinical diagnosis or treatment.
- Inferring sensitive personal attributes from cognitive data.
- Publishing personal cognitive profiles by default.
- Treating generated associations as verified facts.
- Assuming biological and computational processes are equivalent.

## Design principles

- Scientific constructs must have operational definitions.
- Every data point should retain its provenance and relevant context.
- Uncertainty and disagreement must be representable.
- Memory update operations should be inspectable and testable.
- Benchmarks should avoid leaking evaluation data into training.
- Baselines should be simple, documented, and reproducible.
- Privacy and consent are prerequisites for human-subject datasets.

## Intended outputs

The project should produce a vocabulary, ontology, protocols, dataset specifications, benchmark suites, reference implementations, experiment reports, and community-reviewed research notes.

## Success criteria

OCM succeeds when independent contributors can:
- use the same definitions for core constructs;
- reproduce at least one published protocol;
- compare at least two memory approaches on a common benchmark;
- trace a stored item to its source and transformation history;
- understand the consent, access, and licensing status of each dataset.
