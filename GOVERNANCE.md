# Governance

## Status

This document defines a provisional governance model for the early OCM project. It should be reviewed publicly and revised as the community grows.

## Principles

- Scientific integrity and transparency.
- Open participation and respectful disagreement.
- Clear separation of project stewardship and scientific claims.
- Documented decisions and conflicts of interest.
- Privacy, consent, and responsible data stewardship.
- Reproducibility and acknowledgment of contributions.

## Roles

### Maintainers
Maintain repository health, review routine contributions, manage releases, and enforce documented project policies. Maintainers should explain significant decisions.

### Research reviewers
Review scientific proposals for definitions, evidence, methodology, limitations, and reproducibility. A reviewer provides an assessment, not an assertion of institutional or journal-level peer review.

### Contributors
Propose, implement, review, replicate, and document work under the contribution guidelines.

### Dataset stewards
For each dataset, designated stewards maintain provenance, consent records, access rules, licensing, versioning, and incident response. Dataset stewards may restrict access or pause distribution if a credible privacy or rights concern arises.

## Decision process

- Routine documentation and maintenance changes may be merged by maintainers.
- Changes to scientific definitions, protocols, benchmark rules, and dataset policies should receive substantive review.
- Major policy changes should be proposed publicly, discussed, and recorded in a decision record.
- Where consensus is not reached, maintainers should document the alternatives and rationale for the decision.
- Conflicts of interest must be disclosed during review.

## Scientific review

Scientific review should consider:
1. Clarity and operationalization.
2. Quality and relevance of cited evidence.
3. Methodological validity.
4. Baselines and evaluation design.
5. Reproducibility.
6. Limitations, uncertainty, and alternative explanations.
7. Ethical and privacy implications.

## Data governance

Open-source code does not imply open access to all data. Human-subject data requires an appropriate consent and ethics process. Personal cognitive records remain private by default. Dataset stewards must document the rights and conditions governing each dataset.

## Amendments

Governance changes should be proposed through an issue or pull request and recorded with rationale, date, and review outcome.
