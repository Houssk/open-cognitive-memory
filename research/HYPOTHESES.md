# Initial Research Hypotheses

These hypotheses are provisional. They must be refined into falsifiable statements with operational definitions and preregisterable methods where appropriate.

## H1 — Associative information
Free-association observations may capture aspects of a participant's knowledge organization that are not captured by standard topic or preference questionnaires.

Possible tests: incremental predictive value, construct validity, test–retest reliability, and sensitivity to context.

## H2 — Longitudinal structure
Repeated association measurements may reveal changes in an individual's cognitive graph beyond expected measurement noise.

Possible tests: temporal stability, change-point detection, graph similarity, and association with independently measured changes.

## H3 — Consolidation
Explicit consolidation policies may improve selected memory-system outcomes compared with simple append-only or retrieval-only baselines.

Possible outcomes: retrieval quality, consistency, redundancy, latency, and storage cost.

## H4 — Human–LLM association differences
Humans and language models may exhibit different association distributions under carefully matched tasks.

Possible tests: semantic distance, distributional diversity, response entropy, temporal consistency, and context sensitivity.

## H5 — Provenance
Representations that preserve source and transformation history may support more reliable downstream use than representations without provenance.

Possible tests: source attribution, error tracing, contradiction handling, and calibrated abstention.

## H6 — Continual learning trade-offs
Memory update strategies may differ in their balance of new-information acquisition, retention, interference, and computational cost.

Possible tests: retention curves, backward/forward interference, update latency, and resource use.

## Caution

A null result is informative. Do not interpret a graph pattern as a stable personal trait without validation. Avoid sensitive inference and avoid conflating statistical predictability with psychological validity.
